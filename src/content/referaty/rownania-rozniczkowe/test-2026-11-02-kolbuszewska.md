We consider the parabolic–elliptic Keller–Segel system with a logistic source $\mu u(1-u)$ in a bounded domain of $\mathbb{R}^n$, $n \ge 3$. It is known that for $\mu$ large enough the logistic damping prevents aggregation and all solutions are global and bounded, whereas without it radially symmetric solutions with large mass concentrate in finite time. We show that in the intermediate regime the dichotomy is governed by a critical Morrey-type norm of the initial datum: below a threshold solutions exist globally, while for a class of radial data above it blow-up occurs in finite time, despite the damping. The proofs combine comparison arguments for the cumulated mass function with new weighted energy estimates.
